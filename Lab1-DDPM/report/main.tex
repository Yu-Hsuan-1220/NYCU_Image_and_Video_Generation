\section{Introduction}

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) generate data by learning to reverse a gradual
noising procedure: a forward process slowly corrupts a data sample into pure Gaussian noise, and a
neural network is trained to undo that corruption step by step, so that new samples can be produced
by denoising from random noise.

In this lab, I implement DDPM from scratch and study it at two different scales. Task 1 models a 2D
Swiss Roll distribution, where the sampling process can be plotted directly and thus serves as a
sanity check for the implementation. Task 2 scales the same formulation up to image generation, and
further explores how different beta schedulers and prediction targets affect the denoising process
and the quality of the generated samples.


\section{Task 1 - Swiss Roll}

In this task, I model a 2D Swiss Roll distribution with a DDPM. I implement \texttt{SimpleNet}, a
time-conditioned network that predicts the noise added to a sample, together with the core routines
of the algorithm: \texttt{q\_sample} for the forward process, \texttt{p\_sample} and
\texttt{p\_sample\_loop} for the reverse process, and the training loss. Since the data is only two
dimensional, both the forward corruption and the learned distribution can be visualized directly.

\section{Task 2 - Image Generation}

This task applies the same diffusion formulation to a much higher-dimensional distribution: AFHQ
animal-face images at a resolution of $64 \times 64$, using a UNet as the prediction network. Besides
the components of Task 1, I additionally implement three beta schedulers (linear, quadratic, cosine)
and three prediction targets (noise, $\mathbf{x}_0$, mean). I then train the model under different
scheduler and predictor combinations, observe the denoising trajectories, and evaluate the sample
quality with FID.
